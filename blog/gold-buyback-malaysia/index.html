What Is a Gold Buyback Program?

A gold buyback program is a seller's commitment to buy gold back from customers later. It can be as simple as a published daily buyback rate, or a written contract with fixed terms — for example a set price or premium after a minimum holding period. Outside a program, "buyback" just means selling your gold to a dealer at whatever rate it offers that day. For how buyback rates compare with the market price, see gold buyback vs selling at market price.

How Gold Buyback Works in Malaysia

When you bring gold to a dealer for buyback (also called selling gold or redeeming gold), the process follows a standard sequence regardless of which licensed dealer or platform you use:

  1. Assessment: The dealer verifies the purity of your gold — usually via XRF testing for 916 jewellery, or by checking hallmarks on certified bars and coins. Some older dealers still use acid testing, but XRF is the standard at licensed platforms.
  2. Weighing: Your piece is weighed on a certified scale (accurate to 0.01g). This is the foundation of the payout calculation.
  3. Price quote: The dealer quotes a buyback price based on: today's market rate × purity factor × weight, minus their margin/spread. At platforms with a Special BuyBack Contract, this rate is already posted publicly — you can verify it before bringing your gold in.
  4. Transaction: You accept or decline the offer. If you accept, payment is typically cash or bank transfer on the same day.
  5. Receipt: A proper buyback receipt is issued — with your name, the piece's weight, purity, rate, and total paid. Keep this for tax and zakat documentation.

How the Buyback Price Is Calculated

The Buyback Formula
Buyback = Weight (g) × Purity factor × Market price × (1 − Dealer margin)
Example: 10g of 916 gold at RM 400/g with 3% margin:
10 × 0.916 × 400 × 0.97 = RM 3,558
The posted "sell price" would be ~RM 3,664 (10 × 0.916 × 400). The spread is the difference — RM 106 or about 2.9%. This is the dealer's margin for taking the risk of reselling your piece.

Tips to Get the Best Buyback Value

📅
Check the gold price before selling. Gold prices fluctuate daily. Avoid selling during a price dip if you can wait — even a few weeks can mean a meaningfully higher payout. Use FGJAM or any licensed dealer's daily posted rate as your reference.
🏪
Compare at least two buyers. Even within licensed dealers, margins vary. Getting a quote from your platform (if you have an SBC) and one local jewellery shop takes 15 minutes and can net you a better price.
📄
Keep your original receipt. Having the original purchase receipt (showing weight, purity, and date) reduces the time spent on reassessment and may give you more negotiating credibility with a dealer.
🔬
Know your piece's weight. Weigh it yourself on a kitchen scale (not precise, but useful as a sanity check) before going to a dealer. If the dealer's weight differs significantly, ask why — scales at licensed dealers are certified, but it helps to have a reference.
🛡️
Only sell to SSM-registered dealers. Unlicensed buyers may offer a higher price verbally but create problems with documentation, payment, or recourse if something goes wrong.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a gold buyback program?
A gold buyback program is a seller's commitment to buy gold back from its customers — at a published daily rate, or under a written contract with fixed terms such as a set price after a holding period. Always read how the price is calculated and any deductions before you buy.
How does gold buyback work in Malaysia?
The dealer XRF-tests your gold to confirm purity, weighs it, then quotes a buyback price based on the day's market rate minus their spread (usually 2–5%). At platforms with a Special BuyBack Contract, the rate is published openly before you bring your gold in. Payment is typically same-day, cash or bank transfer.
What is a good gold buyback rate in Malaysia?
A good rate is within 2–5% of the current FGJAM market price. Licensed platforms with a Special BuyBack Contract typically sit at the lower end of this spread — often 2–3%. Traditional jewellery shops may apply higher deductions, particularly for older designs. Always compare against the FGJAM reference before accepting any offer.
What is a Special BuyBack Contract (SBC) for gold in Malaysia?
An SBC is a formal agreement where a licensed gold platform commits to buying back your gold at the posted market rate on any day you choose to sell. It eliminates the need to negotiate on the day, removes design-based deductions, and gives you a clear exit strategy when you buy. KESS/SSGC's SBC posts both the daily buy and sell rate publicly.
Can I sell gold at any jewellery shop in Malaysia?
Most licensed jewellery shops will buy gold, but rates and conditions vary. Some only accept pieces from their own stock. Some apply higher margins for older designs. Shops don't publish their buyback rates in advance, so you'll need to visit or call to get a quote. For the most transparent, pre-agreed buyback, an SBC platform is better.
Does the design or condition of preloved gold affect its buyback price in Malaysia?
At SBC platforms like KESS/SSGC, no — buyback is calculated purely on gold weight × purity × market rate. At traditional jewellery shops, yes — an outdated design may fetch less because the shop cannot resell it quickly. Physical damage that reduces the gold's weight will reduce the payout proportionally, since you're being paid for the gold content you're delivering.
