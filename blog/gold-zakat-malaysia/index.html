Gold Zakat Malaysia 2026 — How Much, Nisab, and How to Calculate

The nisab for gold zakat is 85g of pure gold. The rate is 2.5%. It applies after one full hijri year of ownership. Here's the complete step-by-step guide — including how to handle 916 jewellery and platform savings.

Quick summary: If you hold gold worth 85g or more in pure gold terms and have owned it for one hijri year, you owe 2.5% zakat on its total market value. Worn jewellery is exempt under the Shafi'i school (the dominant position in Malaysia). Investment gold and platform savings are not.

The 3 Conditions for Gold Zakat

  1. Nisab: Total gold you own (excluding regularly worn jewellery under Shafi'i ruling) must equal or exceed 85g in pure gold (24K/999) equivalent. Convert 916 gold at ×0.916, 18K at ×0.75.
  2. Haul: You must have owned the gold continuously for one full hijri year — 354 days. The clock starts when your holdings first reach the nisab threshold, not when you first bought gold.
  3. Full ownership: The gold must be freely yours — not pledged as collateral on an active loan. Gold used as security for a financing is not in your full possession.

The Formula

Zakat = 2.5% × (total weight in grams × market price per gram on haul date)
Use the market price on the haul date — not the original purchase price

If gold prices have risen since you bought, your zakat will be higher. If they've fallen, it'll be lower. This is intentional — zakat is on current wealth, not historical cost.

Is Worn Jewellery Subject to Zakat?

This is a genuine point of scholarly disagreement (ikhtilaf). Both positions have strong evidential backing:

Shafi'i School (Dominant in Malaysia)
Gold jewellery worn regularly for personal adornment is not subject to zakat. This is the position of Lembaga Zakat Selangor and most Malaysian state zakat authorities.
Hanafi School (More Precautionary)
All gold — worn or stored — is subject to zakat without exception. This is the more cautious position and is recommended for those who want to err on the side of caution.
Practical note: Most Malaysian state zakat bodies follow the Shafi'i position — everyday jewellery is exempt. However, gold bought as an investment (even in jewellery form), gold stored in a safe, or gold held at a platform like KESS/SSGC is unambiguously subject to zakat under both schools if nisab and haul are met.

Where to Pay Gold Zakat in Malaysia

Pay to the zakat body (Majlis Agama Islam) of the state where you reside. All major authorities offer online payment:

  • Selangor: Lembaga Zakat Selangor — ezakat.com.my
  • Kuala Lumpur / Putrajaya: MAIWP — maiwp.gov.my
  • Kedah: Majlis Agama Islam Kedah — maik.gov.my
  • Penang: Majlis Agama Islam Pulau Pinang — maipenang.gov.my
  • Johor, Perak, Pahang: Contact the respective Majlis Agama Islam negeri

Non-citizen Muslims residing in Malaysia (including Indonesian workers) may pay to the zakat authority of their state of residence.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the nisab for gold zakat in Malaysia 2026?
The nisab is 85 grams of pure gold (24K/999). Convert other purity levels to find the pure equivalent: 916 gold × 0.916, 18K × 0.75. If your total pure gold equivalent reaches 85g and you've held it for one hijri year, zakat of 2.5% is due on the total market value.
How do I calculate zakat on 916 gold in Malaysia?
Multiply the weight by 0.916 to get the pure gold equivalent. If that equals or exceeds 85g and you've held it for a hijri year, pay 2.5% of the market value of your total gold holdings on the haul date. Example: 100g of 916 gold at RM 400/gram = RM 40,000 total value → zakat = RM 1,000.
Is gold jewellery that I wear subject to zakat in Malaysia?
Under the Shafi'i school (dominant in Malaysia), daily-use jewellery is exempt. Gold stored as an investment, kept in a safe, or held at a platform like KESS/SSGC is not exempt — it's zakatable if it meets the nisab and haul conditions.
Where do I pay gold zakat in Malaysia?
Pay to the Majlis Agama Islam of your state of residence. All major state zakat authorities in Malaysia offer online payment at their respective websites.
Does gold held at KESS or SSGC count for zakat?
Yes. Gold held at licensed platforms counts as your property. Add it to any other gold you own to determine if you've reached the 85g nisab. If yes, and you've held the total for one hijri year, 2.5% zakat is due on the combined market value.
