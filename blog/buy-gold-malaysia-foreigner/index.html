Is It Legal for Foreigners to Buy Gold in Malaysia?

Malaysia places no legal restriction on foreigners purchasing gold. Whether you are an expatriate on an Employment Pass, a tourist on a 30-day visa exemption, or a Malaysia My Second Home (MM2H) visa holder, you may purchase gold jewellery, gold bars, or join a gold savings platform — provided you meet the platform's own membership requirements.

Malaysia does not currently impose any gold export restriction on personal amounts. What matters is your destination country's import rules (see FAQ below).

Step-by-Step: Buying Gold at a Malaysian Shop

For a straightforward walk-in purchase — the most common experience for tourists and short-stay expats:

  1. Check the live gold price

    Malaysian gold prices track the global spot price. Check FGJAM (Federation of Goldsmiths and Jewellers Association of Malaysia) or the shop's posted price. Prices are quoted per gram in MYR.

  2. Choose your gold type

    916 for jewellery. 999/999.9 for bars and coins. For the best value, ask about preloved or trade-in stock — same purity, lower total cost because there is no making charge.

  3. Verify the hallmark

    All legitimate gold in Malaysia carries a stamped purity mark (916, 999, etc.) and usually the dealer's mark. Ask to see this before purchasing.

  4. Request a formal receipt

    Always get a printed receipt showing the item description, weight in grams, purity, price per gram, total price, and the shop's SSM registration. This is your proof of purchase and essential for any future resale.

  5. Understand your currency situation

    Payment is in MYR. Most larger shops accept credit cards; smaller shops prefer cash. Currency exchange counters are widely available in shopping malls near gold shops.

Preloved tip: Buying 916 preloved (secondhand) gold eliminates the making charge entirely. A 10-gram preloved 916 chain may cost RM400–500 less than the same weight in new stock — same purity, same resale value.

Taking Gold Out of Malaysia — What You Need to Know

Malaysia does not restrict the export of personal gold. However, two things apply:

  • You must declare gold at customs if the total value exceeds MYR 10,000 (or equivalent) when departing Malaysia — standard currency/valuable goods declaration form
  • Your destination country's import rules are your main constraint — most countries have a threshold above which gold must be declared (e.g., USD 10,000 equivalent in the US)
  • Gold jewellery is typically treated differently from gold bars at most customs — check specifically for each category
  • Keep your purchase receipts — these prove the gold is legitimately purchased, not smuggled

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreigners legally buy gold in Malaysia?
Yes. There are no legal restrictions. Foreigners can purchase gold jewellery, bars, and join savings platforms with a valid passport and, for some platforms, a Malaysian bank account.
What ID do I need to buy gold in Malaysia as a foreigner?
A valid passport is accepted everywhere. For registered savings platforms, you typically also need an Employment Pass, MM2H card, or similar long-term residency document, plus a Malaysian bank account.
Can I open a KESS/SSGC gold savings account as a foreigner?
Yes — KESS/SSGC accepts foreign nationals with valid Malaysian residency documentation (Employment Pass, MM2H, long-term passes) and a Malaysian bank account. Contact an SSGC agent to verify current requirements.
Can I bring the gold I buy in Malaysia back to my home country?
Malaysia does not restrict gold export for personal amounts. Your home country's import rules apply on arrival. Keep your purchase receipts. Declare if above your destination country's threshold.
Is gold cheaper in Malaysia than other countries?
Gold tracks global spot price in Malaysia, but making charges on 916 jewellery are generally lower than in Singapore or the UK. Preloved gold (no making charge at all) is a unique advantage of the Malaysian market. Currency advantage depends on your home currency vs MYR.
Do I pay tax when buying or selling gold in Malaysia as a foreigner?
Investment gold (999 and above) is SST-exempt. 916 jewellery carries a small sales tax. There is no capital gains tax on gold for individuals in Malaysia. Your home country may tax overseas gains — Malaysia will not withhold anything.
