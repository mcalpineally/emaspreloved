How to Check if Gold Is Real in Malaysia

Fake and gold-plated pieces are now sold openly online. Here is what actually tells you whether gold is real — and which popular home tests are useless.

Updated November 2026 8 min read By Alpian Ali
The short version: a stamp is a claim, not proof. The reliable check is an XRF test at a licensed dealer, backed by a receipt that states weight and purity. Home tests can catch obvious fakes but cannot confirm purity.

Step 1 — Read the Stamp

Real gold jewellery in Malaysia is normally stamped with its purity in parts per thousand: 916 (22K), 999 (24K), 750 (18K) or 375 (9K). Look inside rings, on clasps, on pendant bails and on the inside of bangles. Many pieces also carry a maker's mark.

Watch for marks that sound like gold but are not:

  • GP, GF, GEP, HGE, RGP — gold plated, gold filled or electroplated
  • "Plated", "salut", "sadur" — a thin gold layer over another metal
  • "Emas bungkus" / gold-wrapped — marketing terms for non-solid gold

A stamp can be faked, so treat it as the starting point only.

Step 2 — Insist on a Proper Receipt

Malaysian rules on articles made of precious metals require sellers to issue a receipt describing what you bought, and gold-plated items must be clearly labelled "plated". The Domestic Trade Ministry (KPDN) has warned online sellers that misleading gold labels can bring fines of up to RM500,000 for repeat corporate offenders, and that uncertified scales fall under the Weights and Measures Act.

A useful receipt shows:

  • Seller's registered business name and address
  • Item description, purity (e.g. 916) and exact weight in grams
  • Price per gram and any workmanship fee shown separately
  • Date — and, for preloved items, whether the piece was XRF-tested

No receipt, no purchase. It is also your proof when you sell or trade the gold in later.

Step 3 — XRF Test (the Reliable One)

An XRF (X-ray fluorescence) analyser reads the metal composition of a piece in about a minute without scratching or cutting it. Licensed gold shops and preloved gold platforms use it routinely. Ask to see the reading on the screen — it should show a gold percentage close to the stamp (around 91–92% for 916).

Limitation: XRF reads the surface layer. A thick plating over a base-metal core can fool it. Reputable dealers combine XRF with weight, density and experience, and heavy suspicious pieces may need a fire assay or a cut test.

How to Verify Gold Purity at Home — What Works and What Does Not

You cannot measure exact purity at home — only a lab method such as XRF or fire assay can. What home tests can do is flag pieces that are clearly not solid gold.

Red Flags When Buying Gold Online

  • Price per gram well below the day's gold price — no one sells real gold at a loss.
  • Live-stream sellers who will not state purity and weight in writing.
  • "Looks exactly like 916" or "same as real gold" wording.
  • No business registration, no receipt, payment only to a personal account.
  • Refusal to let you test the piece or return it if it fails a test.

What to Do If You Bought Fake Gold

  1. Keep everything: receipt, chat logs, payment proof, listing screenshots.
  2. Get a written XRF result from a licensed gold shop.
  3. Contact the seller in writing asking for a refund.
  4. File a complaint with KPDN, or take a claim to the Tribunal for Consumer Claims Malaysia.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I verify gold purity at home?
Not precisely. Home checks — a strong magnet, looking for different colour at worn edges, weighing against the receipt, and a density test — can expose obvious fakes. Exact purity needs an XRF test or assay at a licensed dealer.
What is the most reliable way to check gold in Malaysia?
An XRF test at a licensed gold dealer, combined with a receipt stating the weight and purity. XRF is fast and does not damage the piece.
Does a 916 stamp guarantee the gold is real?
No. A stamp is the seller's claim and can be faked. Treat it as a first check, then confirm with XRF and a proper receipt.
Does the magnet test work on gold?
Only partly. Real gold is not magnetic, so a piece that sticks to a strong magnet is not solid gold. But many fakes use non-magnetic metals, so passing the magnet test proves little.
What does GP or HGE mean on jewellery?
GP means gold plated and HGE means heavy gold electroplate. Both mean a thin gold layer over another metal — not solid gold.
Where can I report a seller who sold me fake gold?
You can complain to the Domestic Trade and Cost of Living Ministry (KPDN) or file a claim at the Tribunal for Consumer Claims Malaysia. Keep your receipt, chats and payment proof.
