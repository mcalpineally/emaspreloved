How the 916 Gold Price is Calculated

Malaysia's 916 gold price is not set arbitrarily. It is derived from a chain of calculations that connect directly to the global gold market:

The Price Chain
1. International spot price (USD/troy oz) → XAU
2. Convert to grams: ÷ 31.1035 → USD per gram (pure gold)
3. Convert to Ringgit: × USD/MYR rate → RM per gram (pure gold)
4. Adjust for 916 purity: × 0.916 → RM per gram (916 gold)
5. FGJAM publishes this as the daily benchmark rate
Individual dealers may add a small dealer margin of RM 1–5/gram on top of FGJAM. New jewellery adds fabrication fees (upah) of RM 15–50/gram.

This means the 916 price moves every day — sometimes multiple times intraday at platforms offering live pricing — in response to changes in both the global gold price and the USD/MYR exchange rate. A weakening Ringgit makes gold more expensive even when the international spot price hasn't moved.

The Buy Price vs Sell Price — Understanding the Spread

Every licensed gold dealer in Malaysia posts two prices:

Buy Price (Harga Beli)
What the dealer pays YOU when you bring in gold to sell. This is always lower than the sell price. It's also called the buyback price.
Sell Price (Harga Jual)
What YOU pay the dealer when you buy gold from them. This is always higher. For new jewellery, add fabrication fees on top.

The gap between buy and sell prices is the dealer's margin. For 916 gold jewellery at licensed dealers, this spread is typically RM 10–20 per gram. Transparent platforms — especially those offering a Special BuyBack Contract — post both prices publicly so buyers know the buyback value at the moment of purchase.

How to Read a Gold Price Listing

When you see a 916 gold price posted by a dealer or platform, check for these elements:

  • Purity stated: Should specify 916 (or 22K), not just "gold"
  • Price per gram: Always confirm it's per gram, not per tola or mace (older units)
  • Buy and sell both shown: A transparent dealer posts both so you know the spread before transacting
  • Date/time stamp: Gold prices are live — an undated post may be hours or days old
  • FGJAM reference: Better dealers mention whether their price tracks FGJAM or their own market rate
  • No buyback policy stated: Ask explicitly what they'll pay you if you sell back
  • Price far below market: May indicate the piece is not 916, or significant damage affects value
  • No XRF certificate: Always confirm that 916 gold has been XRF-tested before buying

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 916 gold price in Malaysia today?
The 916 gold price changes daily with the international gold market and the USD/MYR exchange rate. For the current rate, check FGJAM (fgjam.com.my) or any licensed dealer's posted price. As a reference, 916 gold is 91.6% the price of pure (999.9) gold — if pure gold is at RM 440/gram, then 916 is at approximately RM 403/gram.
What is the difference between 916 gold buy price and sell price in Malaysia?
The buy price is what a dealer pays you when you sell your gold to them. The sell price is what you pay the dealer when you buy. The gap (spread) is their margin — typically RM 10–20 per gram for 916 jewellery at licensed dealers. At transparent platforms, both prices are posted publicly.
How is the 916 gold price in Malaysia calculated?
Start with the international spot price in USD/troy oz, divide by 31.1035 to get per gram, multiply by the USD/MYR rate to get RM, then multiply by 0.916 for 916 purity. FGJAM publishes this daily as the official benchmark. Dealers add a small margin; new jewellery adds fabrication fees (upah) of RM 15–50 per gram.
Why is preloved 916 gold cheaper than new 916 gold?
Preloved gold is priced on the gold content value alone — no fabrication fees, since the original buyer paid those. The gold itself is XRF-verified and identical in purity to new jewellery. Buying preloved means you're closer to the true metal value from day one.
What is FGJAM and why does it matter for 916 gold prices?
FGJAM (Federation of Goldsmiths and Jewellers Association of Malaysia) publishes the official daily reference price for 916 gold in Malaysia. It is the benchmark that licensed dealers and gold platforms use to set their buying and selling prices. It is published weekday mornings based on the previous day's closing London gold price converted to MYR.
