What the Number "916" Actually Means

Gold purity is expressed in parts per thousand. The number on a gold item tells you how many parts out of 1,000 are pure gold:

  • 916 → 916 parts per 1,000 are gold = 91.6% pure
  • 999 → 999 parts per 1,000 are gold = 99.9% pure
  • 750 → 750 parts per 1,000 are gold = 75% pure (18-karat)

The remaining percentage (8.4% for 916 gold) is an alloy — typically copper, silver, or zinc — mixed in to make the gold harder and more suitable for jewellery.

The 916 Hallmark — What to Look For

Every legitimate 916 gold item in Malaysia carries a stamped hallmark. Here is where to find it:

  • Rings: stamped on the inner band
  • Chains & necklaces: stamped on the clasp or on a small tag attached to the clasp
  • Bangles & bracelets: stamped on the inner surface
  • Pendants: stamped on the bail (the loop that the chain passes through)
  • Earrings: stamped on the post or clasp

The hallmark will show "916" and may also include the dealer's mark. If you cannot find the stamp, ask the shop to show you — legitimate dealers are always transparent about this.

XRF testing: When in doubt, any licensed dealer with an XRF (X-ray fluorescence) machine can test gold purity in seconds without damaging the piece. KESS/SSGC branches offer XRF testing. This is the most reliable verification method.

Why Does Malaysia Use 916 (22-Karat) as Its Standard?

916 gold became dominant in Malaysia and Southeast Asia because it offers an optimal balance for the region's jewellery tradition:

  • High purity (91.6%) means high intrinsic gold value — close to pure gold
  • The 8.4% alloy adds enough hardness for practical jewellery that holds its shape over decades
  • The colour is a rich, recognisable gold — culturally associated with prosperity and celebration
  • Historically, 22-karat was the trade standard across the Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia before international karat notation became standard

916 Preloved Gold — The Investment Argument

New 916 gold jewellery carries a making charge of RM15–50 per gram. This is the craftsman's fee for shaping the gold — and you do not recover it on resale. The buyback price is based on the gold's weight and purity, not the craftsmanship.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does 916 gold mean in Malaysia?
916 gold is 91.6% pure gold — equivalent to 22-karat. The remaining 8.4% is alloy for durability. It is the most common jewellery standard in Malaysian gold shops.
Is 916 gold real gold?
Yes. 916 gold is real gold — 91.6% of its weight is pure gold. The small alloy content makes it stronger for jewellery. It is a fully recognised international standard (22-karat).
What is the difference between 916 and 999 gold?
916 is 91.6% pure (22-karat), used mainly for jewellery. 999 is 99.9% pure (24-karat), used for bars and investment products. 999 is purer and softer; 916 is harder and more durable. Both are widely accepted for resale in Malaysia.
How do I identify 916 gold?
Look for the "916" stamp on the piece — inside a ring, on a clasp, or on a pendant bail. For certainty, an XRF machine test at a licensed dealer confirms purity without damaging the item.
Can I sell 916 gold back to any gold shop in Malaysia?
Yes. 916 gold is universally accepted for buyback or trade-in at all licensed gold shops. The buyback price is based on the prevailing market rate for 916 gold per gram.
Is 916 gold good for investment?
New 916 jewellery is less investment-efficient than 999 bars due to making charges. Preloved 916 gold — bought without paying making charges — is an efficient investment that tracks gold prices from day one with no built-in loss.
