Gold Buyback vs Selling at Market Price

The gold price you see online is a reference, not an offer. Here is how that price becomes the amount you are actually paid — and how a buyback programme changes the picture.

Updated November 2026 7 min read By Alpian Ali
In one line: the market (spot) price is what gold trades at in bulk between big players. A buyback rate is what a specific dealer will pay you today. The gap between them is the dealer's spread.

Prices used in this article: in late November 2026 the reference spot price was about RM486/g for 999 and RM445/g for 916, while jewellery shops were selling at roughly RM565/g (916) and RM615/g (999). Prices change every day — see today's price.

Why You Do Not Get the Market Price

A dealer that buys your gold has to test it, hold it, possibly refine it, and carry the risk that prices fall before it resells. The spread pays for that. For jewelry, purity also matters: 916 is priced at about 91.6% of the pure-gold rate.

What a Buyback Programme Adds

Some sellers commit, at the time you buy, to buying the gold back from you later. Terms vary widely:

  • Posted-rate buyback — the seller will buy back any day at its published buyback rate.
  • Fixed-term buyback — the seller agrees to a set price or premium if you sell back on or after a set date.
  • Trade-in — your gold's value is credited towards another piece.

A programme gives you certainty that someone will buy, and sometimes a better rate than walking into a random shop. It does not remove price risk — if the gold price falls, a posted-rate buyback falls with it.

What to Check in Any Buyback Terms

  • How the buyback price is calculated, in writing
  • Any deductions — for wear, stones, or selling early
  • Lock-in periods or dates when the terms apply
  • Who holds the gold until you sell — you or the seller
  • How and when you are paid
  • What happens if the seller closes or changes its terms

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the gold buyback price lower than the market price?
The market (spot) price is a wholesale reference. Dealers pay less to cover testing, holding, refining and price risk. The difference is the dealer's spread.
Can I sell my gold at the spot price?
Rarely. Individuals sell at a dealer's buyback rate. A private sale may come closer to spot but carries more risk.
What is a gold buyback programme?
A commitment by the seller to buy gold back from you later — at its posted rate, at a set price after a fixed term, or as a trade-in credit. Terms differ, so read them in writing.
Does a buyback guarantee protect me if gold prices fall?
Usually not. A posted-rate buyback follows the market. Only a fixed-price agreement protects against falls, and those come with conditions.
How do I get the best buyback rate?
Know the day's spot price, the purity and weight of your gold, and compare quotes from two or three dealers before you sell.
