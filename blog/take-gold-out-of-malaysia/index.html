How Much Gold Can You Take Out of Malaysia?

Leaving Malaysia with gold is usually the easy part. The limits that matter are set by the country you land in. Here is what to know before you fly home.

Updated November 2026 8 min read By Alpian Ali
Check before you travel: customs rules change. This guide reflects what official and news sources said in November 2026. Confirm with the customs authority of the country you are entering before you fly.

Leaving Malaysia

We could not find any Malaysian rule that sets a weight or value limit on personal gold jewellery a traveller takes out of the country. Bank Negara Malaysia's published traveller rules cover cash and traveller's cheques, not gold. In practice:

  • Jewellery you wear or carry for personal use is not normally questioned.
  • Keep the purchase receipt stating weight and purity — it proves ownership and value if any officer asks.
  • Large quantities of bars or a commercial amount are a different matter. Ask the Royal Malaysian Customs Department before travelling.
  • Carry gold in your hand luggage, never checked baggage.

Arriving in Indonesia

  • Jewellery worn for personal use in a reasonable amount is generally treated as personal effects.
  • Other goods you bring, including gold bars and unworn jewellery, count towards the passenger allowance of USD 500 per person.
  • Above that, import duty and tax apply on the excess, and you must declare and use the red channel.

Arriving in Singapore

  • GST relief for travellers covers goods up to S$500 if you were away 2 days or more, or S$100 if under 2 days. GST is due on the value above that.
  • Gold jewellery does not qualify as an investment precious metal, so it falls under normal GST rules.
  • Investment-grade gold bars, wafers and approved coins are GST-exempt, but must be declared at the Red Channel. Hand-carried investment gold above 0.5 kg needs a GST Relief Permit.

Arriving in India

India's Baggage Rules 2026 took effect on 2 February 2026:

  • Passengers who have lived abroad for more than one year may bring gold jewellery duty-free up to 40 g for women and 20 g for other passengers, as bona fide baggage.
  • The general duty-free allowance for residents and tourists of Indian origin is ₹75,000; for foreign tourists, ₹25,000.
  • Gold bars and coins are dutiable and must be declared.

Other Destinations

Check the destination country's customs website for its passenger allowance, how it treats jewellery versus bullion, and whether a declaration is needed. Bangladesh, Pakistan, Nepal and the Philippines all have their own limits, and they change often.

Documents to Carry

  • Passport
  • Purchase receipt(s) showing weight, purity, price and the seller's business details
  • Certificate for any bar or coin
  • Photos of the pieces taken before you travel
  • An estimate of the total value in the destination's currency

Practical Tips

  1. Work out the value in USD or the destination currency before you fly, so you know whether you are near the allowance.
  2. If you are over the limit, declare it. Paying duty costs far less than seizure and penalties.
  3. Do not carry gold for other people. Allowances apply to your own belongings.
  4. Wear what you normally wear; pack the rest in hand luggage with the receipts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a limit on taking gold jewellery out of Malaysia?
We could not find a published Malaysian weight or value limit for personal gold jewellery carried by departing travellers. Keep receipts, and check with Royal Malaysian Customs if you are carrying large amounts or bars.
Do I need to declare gold when leaving Malaysia?
Bank Negara Malaysia's traveller declaration rules cover cash and traveller's cheques, not gold. For commercial quantities or bullion, confirm with Malaysian Customs before travelling.
How much gold can I bring into Indonesia from Malaysia?
Jewellery worn for personal use in a reasonable amount is generally treated as personal effects. Other goods, including gold bars and unworn jewellery, count towards the USD 500 per-person allowance; duty and tax apply above it.
How much gold jewellery can I take to India duty-free in 2026?
Under the Baggage Rules 2026, passengers who have lived abroad for more than a year may bring up to 40 g (women) or 20 g (others) of gold jewellery duty-free as bona fide baggage.
Is gold jewellery GST-free when entering Singapore?
No. Jewellery is not treated as investment precious metal, so it falls under the normal traveller GST relief of S$500 (2 days or more away) or S$100 (under 2 days). Investment-grade bars and coins are GST-exempt but must be declared.
