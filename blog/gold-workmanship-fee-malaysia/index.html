Gold Making Charges (Upah) Explained

The price tag on new gold jewellery is gold value plus a fee for making it. When you sell, only the gold value comes back. Here is how that fee works and how to avoid it.

What Are Making Charges on Gold Jewelry?

Making charges — called upah in Malaysia, or the workmanship fee — is what a jewellery shop charges for designing, casting, finishing and selling a piece, on top of the value of the gold itself. In Malaysia it is usually quoted per gram, so a heavier piece carries a bigger total fee.

Some shops show it as a separate line on the receipt; others fold it into a single "price per gram" that sits well above the day's gold price. Either way, you are paying it.

Typical Rates

Type of pieceTypical fee per gram
Plain chains, simple banglesAround RM15–25
Patterned bangles, pendants, ringsAround RM25–50
Intricate, designer or branded collectionsCan be well above RM50
999 bars and wafersSmall premium, not a per-design fee
Preloved jewelleryNone

Ranges vary by shop, region and design. Always ask for the fee to be shown separately.

Why You Lose It When You Sell

When you sell or trade in jewellery, the shop prices it by weight × purity × that day's buyback rate. The design is not paid for, because the shop may melt it or resell it as preloved. So the workmanship fee you paid is a cost you never recover.

Prices used in this article: in late November 2026 the reference spot price was about RM486/g for 999 and RM445/g for 916, while jewellery shops were selling at roughly RM565/g (916) and RM615/g (999). Prices change every day — see today's price.
Example: 20 g new 916 bangleAmount
Gold value (20 g × RM445)RM8,900
Workmanship fee (20 g × RM35)+RM700
You pay (before shop margin)RM9,600
Fee lost when you sellRM700 — about 7% of what you paid

Gold prices need to rise by roughly that percentage before you simply break even.

Four Ways to Avoid or Reduce It

  1. Buy preloved. Second-hand 916 jewellery is sold close to its gold value. The first owner already absorbed the fee. Make sure it is XRF-tested and comes with a receipt — see how to check real gold.
  2. Buy bars or wafers for storing value. 999 bars carry a small premium rather than a design fee.
  3. Choose simpler designs. Plain chains and bangles carry lower fees than detailed pieces.
  4. Ask for the fee in writing. Compare shops on the fee per gram, not only the total.

When Paying the Fee Is Fine

If you are buying a wedding set, a gift or a design you love and will keep for years, the fee is part of the price of beautiful work. It only becomes a problem when you treat new jewellery as savings and expect to sell it later without a loss.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are making charges on gold jewelry?
Making charges are the fee a jeweler adds on top of the gold value for designing and crafting a piece. In Malaysia they are called upah and are usually quoted per gram — around RM15–50/g for new 916 jewelry. They are not returned when you sell.
What is upah in gold jewellery?
Upah is the workmanship or making fee a jewellery shop charges on top of the gold value, usually quoted per gram.
How much is the gold workmanship fee in Malaysia?
For new 916 jewellery it is typically around RM15–50 per gram, depending on design. Intricate or branded pieces can cost more.
Do I get the workmanship fee back when I sell?
No. Shops pay for weight and purity at the buyback rate. The fee for the design is not returned.
Does preloved gold have a workmanship fee?
Preloved jewellery is sold close to its gold value without a new workmanship fee, because the first owner already paid it.
Do gold bars have a workmanship fee?
Bars carry a small premium over the gold price rather than a design fee, which is why they resell closer to what you paid.
