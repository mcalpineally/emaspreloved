Gold purity and product type

Both KESS and Public Gold deal in 999.9 pure gold — the highest commercial grade available, equivalent to 24 karat. This is investment-grade gold, the same standard used by central banks and the LBMA (London Bullion Market Association).

The difference is in the product form:

  • KESS: Deals exclusively in 999.9 gold, stored at a branch on your behalf. You hold the account; the gold is stored centrally. You do not take physical delivery during your savings period.
  • Public Gold: Sells physical gold bars and coins you take home, plus a range of 916 jewellery and gold dinar. You hold the gold yourself — which means you're responsible for storage and security.
  • Ar-Rahnu: Primarily works with 916 gold jewellery (91.6% pure, 22 karat) — the common standard in Malaysian gold jewellery. Some Ar-Rahnu counters accept 999 gold bars from recognised brands.

916 vs 999.9: Malaysian gold jewellery is typically 916 (22 karat). Investment gold bars and coins are 999 or 999.9 (24 karat). When comparing buyback prices, always check which purity is being referenced — a higher gram price on 916 gold is not the same as the same price on 999.9.

Buyback: how each platform handles it

This is where the three options differ most significantly — and where the comparison between KESS and Public Gold is most meaningful.

KESS buyback: standard + SBB premium

KESS offers two buyback modes:

  1. Standard buyback — sell your gold back to KESS at the prevailing market price, similar to any gold dealer.
  2. Special BuyBack (SBB) — a fixed-term contract (typically 6 months) where KESS commits to buying your gold back at a guaranteed premium above spot price. The SBB Umrah Package, for example, offers a premium of USD 3.67 per gram over spot — on a 50g holding, that's USD 183.50 (approximately RM800+) above market price.

Public Gold buyback: market rate

Public Gold buys back gold at the prevailing market price at the time of sale. There is no guaranteed premium above spot. The advantage: you can sell whenever you want, at any Public Gold counter, without committing to a contract term.

Ar-Rahnu: redemption, not buyback

Ar-Rahnu doesn't buy your gold — it lends against it. When your loan term ends, you repay the loan principal plus the ujrah (safekeeping fee) and your gold is returned to you. If you can't repay, the gold is auctioned at market price and any surplus above the loan amount is returned to you.

KESS's SBB premium — what it means in practice

The SBB feature is KESS's most distinctive offering and deserves a clear worked example.

SBB Umrah Package worked example (illustrative):

Member holds 50g of 999.9 gold through KESS. At a spot price of RM430/gram, the market value of 50g = RM21,500.

Under SBB Umrah: KESS buys back at spot + USD 3.67/gram premium. At USD/MYR = 4.40, the premium per gram = RM16.15. On 50g: total premium = RM807.50.

SBB payout = RM21,500 + RM807.50 = RM22,307.50 — versus RM21,500 at market rate. The premium funds the Umrah package contribution.

Figures are illustrative. Actual spot price and exchange rates vary. The premium amount and terms are set by KESS at contract activation.

The SBB is not available to all members at all times — activation depends on your account standing, your holdings, and the specific SBB option available at that time. It is not a simple "buy gold, get a premium" formula — it requires a commitment to hold for the contract duration (typically 6 months).

Ar-Rahnu's real role (it's not a savings platform)

To be direct: Ar-Rahnu is not a gold savings platform. It is an Islamic pawn service. The correct framing is:

  • KESS and Public Gold are ways to accumulate gold over time.
  • Ar-Rahnu is a way to unlock cash from gold you already have — without selling it.

The two roles are complementary, not competitive. Many serious gold savers in Malaysia use Ar-Rahnu strategically — for example:

  1. Buy physical gold from Public Gold (bars with certificate).
  2. When cash flow is tight, pledge the bars at Ar-Rahnu for a short-term loan.
  3. Repay the loan + ujrah when cash flow improves, reclaim the gold.

This strategy lets you hold gold long-term without selling it every time you need liquidity.

Can you use Ar-Rahnu with KESS gold? Generally not in its current form — KESS gold is stored at a branch under your account, not in your physical possession. Ar-Rahnu requires you to bring the physical gold to the counter as collateral. If you want to use Ar-Rahnu strategically, gold bought directly from Public Gold (physical bars with original packaging and cert) is the more Ar-Rahnu-compatible form.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main difference between KESS and Public Gold?

Both deal in 999.9 physical gold. The key differences: KESS offers a Special BuyBack (SBB) scheme with a guaranteed premium above spot price, an internal SCP reward currency (1 SCP = 1 USD), and a community affiliate structure. Public Gold offers a wider product range (bars, coins, dinar, jewellery), established brand recognition, and allows you to take physical possession immediately — with no guaranteed premium, no reward currency, and no community obligation.

Is Ar-Rahnu a way to buy or save gold?

No. Ar-Rahnu is an Islamic pawn service — you bring gold you already own to a counter and receive a cash loan against it. You pay a safekeeping fee (ujrah) and repay the loan to get your gold back. If you don't repay, the gold is auctioned. Ar-Rahnu does not sell you gold and is not a gold savings account.

Is KESS more risky than Public Gold?

They carry different risk profiles. Public Gold gives you physical possession immediately — your risk is mainly personal storage/theft risk. KESS stores your gold at their branch; your risk includes counterparty risk (the platform's operational stability) during the period you hold through them, especially under an SBB contract. Public Gold is older and more established, which many see as lower counterparty risk. KESS is newer but growing, with 30 branches. As always with any financial commitment, verify SSM registration status and understand all terms before committing.

Can I convert KESS gold into Public Gold bars?

Not directly. These are separate platforms with no cross-redemption. If you want to move from KESS to physical gold in hand, you would sell through KESS's buyback (at market rate or SBB if eligible), receive cash, and then separately purchase gold bars from Public Gold or another dealer.

Which is better for a first-time gold buyer in Malaysia?

Depends on your learning style. KESS suits first-timers who benefit from an upline guiding them through the process — you have a support person. Public Gold suits first-timers who want to learn independently and start small (from 1g). Bank gold accounts (Maybank GIA, etc.) are the simplest entry point for absolute beginners since everything is digital — but note they are paper gold, not physical. If physical gold ownership is the goal, both KESS and Public Gold require more commitment than a bank account but deliver true physical gold.

Is the KESS SBB premium guaranteed?

The SBB premium is a contractual commitment by KESS at the time of contract activation — the premium per gram (e.g. USD 3.67/g for the Umrah package) is stated in writing when you sign the contract. It is "guaranteed" in the sense that it is a contractual obligation, not a floating promise. As with any contract, it is subject to the terms and the continuing operation of the platform. Always read the full SBB contract terms with your upline or at a branch before activating.
