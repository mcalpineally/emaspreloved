Saving Gold

Physical Gold vs Gold Savings Account

A gold account lets you buy gold in tiny amounts from your phone. Physical gold puts the metal in your hands. They solve different problems — here is how each works and when to use it.

Updated November 2026 8 min read By Alpian Ali

What Is a Gold Savings Account?

A gold savings account (also called a gold investment account) records your gold in grams instead of ringgit. You buy and sell at the provider's posted prices through an app or branch. The provider holds the gold, or the value of it, on your behalf. Most Malaysian banks offer one, often in conventional and Islamic versions.

  • Buy from small amounts, sometimes around RM10–50
  • Your balance moves with the gold price
  • You sell back to the provider at its buying price
  • Some accounts let you convert grams into physical bars, for a fee
Not deposit-insured: PIDM states that gold-related investment products or accounts are not protected by its deposit insurance. Your gold balance is not treated like a savings deposit.

Physical Gold in Brief

Bars, coins, wafers or jewelry that you own and hold — at home, in a safe deposit box, or stored in your name by a dealer. You sell to any gold shop or dealer that will buy at a fair rate.

The Shariah Question

In Islamic finance, gold is a ribawi item: it should be exchanged hand to hand, with possession taken at the time of sale. Physical gold you receive meets this clearly. For gold accounts, scholars differ — Islamic versions are structured to satisfy these rules, while some scholars still prefer physical possession. If this matters to you, read the product's Shariah statement and ask your bank or a qualified adviser.

Which Should You Choose?

A gold account suits you if…

  • You want to start with very small, regular amounts
  • You value instant buying and selling
  • You do not want to store anything

Physical gold suits you if…

  • You want to own the metal outright, with no provider in between
  • You may need Ar-Rahnu financing against it
  • You want to wear it, gift it or pass it on

Or use both

Many savers build up small monthly amounts in an account and move to physical gold — a wafer or preloved piece — once the balance reaches a few grams. See how to start saving gold with a small amount.

Questions to Ask Before Opening a Gold Account

  • What is the spread between the buying and selling price?
  • Are there account, maintenance or conversion fees?
  • Can I convert to physical gold, and at what cost?
  • Is the gold allocated to me, or is it a claim on the provider?
  • Is there an Islamic version, and what is its structure?

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a gold savings account?
An account that records your gold in grams. You buy and sell at the provider's posted prices, usually in an app, and the provider holds the gold or its value for you.
Is a gold savings account protected by PIDM?
No. PIDM states that gold-related investment products or accounts are not protected by its deposit insurance.
Is physical gold better than a gold account?
Neither is better for everyone. Accounts are cheaper to start and easier to trade; physical gold removes provider risk and can be worn or used as Ar-Rahnu collateral.
Can I convert my gold account into physical gold?
Some providers allow it, usually in fixed bar sizes and for a fee. Check your account terms.
Is a gold savings account Shariah-compliant?
Islamic versions are structured to be Shariah-compliant, but scholars differ on gold held without physical possession. Read the product's Shariah terms and ask a qualified adviser.
