How to Sell Old Gold Jewellery in Malaysia for the Best Price

Broken chains, single earrings and pieces you no longer wear are still worth their gold. Here is how to get a fair price instead of the first number a counter offers.

Updated November 2026 8 min read By Alpian Ali
The formula every shop uses: weight (g) × purity × that day's buyback rate. Know those three numbers before you walk in and you will know whether an offer is fair.

How the Buyback Price Is Worked Out

A shop's buyback rate is below its selling rate — the gap is the shop's margin. Design, brand and the workmanship fee you originally paid are not counted. Damaged, broken or tangled pieces are usually priced the same as intact ones of the same purity, because they will be melted.

Prices used in this article: in late November 2026 the reference spot price was about RM486/g for 999 and RM445/g for 916, while jewellery shops were selling at roughly RM565/g (916) and RM615/g (999). Prices change every day — see today's price.
ExampleCalculation
Old 916 chain and earrings12.4 g
Buyback rate quoted (example)RM420/g for 916
Offer you should expect12.4 × RM420 ≈ RM5,208

If an offer is far below what your own calculation gives, ask the shop to explain each number.

Before You Go: Prepare These

  • Your MyKad or passport — licensed shops record seller details.
  • Original receipts if you have them — they confirm purity and ownership.
  • A rough weight from a kitchen scale, so you can spot a big mismatch.
  • Today's buyback rate from two or three shops or platforms.
  • Pieces sorted by purity (916, 999, 750). Mixed bags sometimes get priced at the lowest purity.

At the Counter: Seven Steps

  1. Ask for the day's buyback rate per gram for each purity before handing anything over.
  2. Watch the piece being weighed on a scale facing you. Ask if the scale is certified.
  3. Ask for an XRF test and see the reading — especially for unstamped pieces.
  4. Ask whether any deduction applies (stones, solder, hollow sections) and how much.
  5. Remove gemstones and non-gold parts first, or ask that their weight is excluded.
  6. Get the offer in writing and compare it with your own calculation.
  7. Get a receipt for the sale showing weight, purity, rate and total.

Trade-In vs Cash

If you want a new-looking piece rather than cash, a trade-in often works better: the value of your old gold goes towards another item. Trading old gold for preloved jewellery is the most efficient swap, because you avoid paying a fresh workmanship fee on the new piece.

Mistakes That Cost You Money

  • Accepting the first offer without knowing the day's rate.
  • Letting the piece be weighed out of your sight.
  • Selling 999 items mixed with 916 at the 916 rate.
  • Leaving stones in and having their weight treated as gold — or deducted generously.
  • Selling in a hurry when a short-term loan (Ar-Rahnu) would have let you keep the gold.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the price of old gold calculated in Malaysia?
Weight in grams × purity × the shop's buyback rate for that day. Design and the original workmanship fee are not counted.
Can I sell broken gold jewellery?
Yes. Broken or tangled pieces are generally valued on weight and purity like intact ones, because they will be melted or repaired.
Do I need the original receipt to sell gold?
It helps but is not always required. Licensed shops will usually test the gold and record your identity card or passport details.
Is it better to trade in or sell for cash?
If you want another piece anyway, trading in towards preloved jewellery avoids paying a new workmanship fee. If you need money, compare cash buyback rates from a few shops.
Why is the buyback price lower than the selling price?
The gap is the shop's margin for testing, handling, refining or reselling. Comparing buyback rates between shops is the best way to narrow it.
